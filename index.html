Thresholds

Read it in 20 seconds

Tickets = how many people bet a side. Money = how many dollars. When money is far behind tickets, the crowd is there and the big bettors are not.

Open → now = where the book started the number and where it is. The book moves it based on the bets it takes.

🪤 RLM = crowd on one side, number moved the other way anyway. The book is betting against its own customers, because someone it respects bet the other side. That is the tell.

Fade = the side opposite the crowd, at today's number. A read, not a lock.

The labels

Strong tellThree or more independent signals agree: DraftKings moved against the crowd, Pinnacle (the sharpest book) moved against it, money trails tickets, Circa's money is on the fade, Kalshi buyers are being sold to.
Solid tellTwo signals agree.
LeanOnly one line move, at DraftKings or at Pinnacle. Real, but thin.
WatchCrowd on one side, money trailing, number has not moved yet. If it moves before kickoff it becomes a fade.
ChalkTickets, money and line all agree. Nothing hiding.
Kicked offGame already started. Pre-game numbers only; never suggested.

Reading the odds

Utah -11Utah must win by 12 or more. Arkansas +11 covers if they lose by 10 or less, or win.
o49.5 / u49.5Both teams' points combined, over or under 49.5.
-400Bet $400 to win $100. The favorite.
+300Bet $100 to win $300. The underdog.

The moneyline chart shows the price as a win-percentage so it fits on one axis: -400 is about 80%.

The three views

CFB / NFL at the top switches the slate; every tab and filter applies to the sport you picked. Spreads within 14 keeps games where the favorite lays two touchdowns or less and looks only at the spread. Big favorites are where the crowd and the books both get lazy, so the tell is worth less there.

Moneylines uses the same games but looks at the win-or-lose price. Everything opens all games and all markets, totals included. Change the 14 under Thresholds on the Board.

The chart

The book's number over time, stepped because lines move in ticks. The solid blue line is DraftKings, the book whose tickets and money you are reading. A dashed teal line is Pinnacle, the sharpest book in the world: when DraftKings lags Pinnacle, DraftKings usually follows. A dotted grey line is the Kalshi price. "Open → now" is the whole path; "Game day" zooms to the 24 hours before kickoff, where most sharp money lands. A red dot marks a move of half a point or more (one point of win-percentage on the moneyline) that went against the crowd; amber went with the crowd.

Columns under the line, when present, are Kalshi contracts bought each hour: blue on the home team, orange on the away team. Kalshi is a regulated exchange, so those are real dollars. Buyers piling into one side while the price moves the other way means someone bigger is selling to them.

Where the numbers come from

Tickets and money: DraftKings, published by Cleatz. Sharp money: Circa Sports, published by VSiN. Openers: Cleatz's "moves since open" and the Scores and Odds open. Line history: DraftKings and Pinnacle numbers this tool snapshots itself from the Odds API, plus the Kalshi price by the hour. Tape: Kalshi fills. Numbers only refresh when the page is republished; the header says how old they are and turns red past six hours.

This is a research tool for a group of friends. It does not place bets and is not advice. One book's splits, a known signal, and a short track record: bet small, track it, decide with data.
